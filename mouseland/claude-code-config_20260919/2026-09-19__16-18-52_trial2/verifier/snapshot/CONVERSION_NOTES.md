### Notes
- **No ΔF/F computation is needed.** The `spk` files already contain Suite2p
  *deconvolved* traces ("All our analyses were based on deconvolved fluorescence
  traces", Methods). `spks` is a list of per-plane `(n, nframes)` float32 arrays.
- **No spike-sorting quality filter exists** (this is imaging, not ephys). The only
  neuron-level curation in the reference code is anatomical:
  `(iarea != -1) & (iarea != 7)` = "exclude neurons from outside of visual cortex".
- Behaviour arrays prefixed `ft_` are sampled **once per neural frame** and are the
  alignment bridge between behaviour and imaging. The reference always truncates them
  with `[:nfr]` where `nfr = spk.shape[1]` (the behaviour arrays are 1 sample longer).
- Key `beh` fields (from the notebook's data dictionary):
  `ntrials`, `Trial_start_time` (corridor entry), `Gray_space_time`, `Trial_end_time`,
  `Corridor_Length`(=60 dm), `Texture_Length`(=40 dm), `Gray_Space_length`(=20 dm),
  `WallName`/`UniqWalls`/`stim_id`, `isRew`, `SoundPos`/`SoundTime`/`SoundFr`,
  `RewPos`/`RewTime`/`RewardFr`, `LickPos`/`LickTime`/`LickFr`/`LickTrind`,
  `ft`, `ft_trInd`, `ft_Pos`, `ft_PosCum`, `ft_move`, `ft_isMoving`, `ft_CorrSpc`,
  `ft_GraySpc`, `ft_WallID`, `ft_RunSpeed`, `StartFr`/`GrayFr`/`EndFr`.
- `beh['stim_id']` is the paper's **canonical stimulus role code**:
  `0:circle1, 1:circle2, 2:leaf1, 3:leaf2, 4:leaf3, 5:leaf1_swap1, 6:leaf1_swap2`.
  It is *role*-based, not name-based (e.g. for mouse TX109 the physical `circle1`
  texture carries `stim_id == 2`, i.e. it played the "leaf1"/rewarded role;
  for rock/wood mice `rock1→0`, `wood1→2`, …). `stim_id == 2` is always the
  rewarded stimulus in task sessions (`utils.Get_dprime_rewPred_neuron`).

### Processing Details
- Temporal alignment for the reference's own figures is mostly **positional**
  (activity interpolated onto 60 spatial bins per trial) or **event-locked**
  (`spk_2_cue`, `spk_2_firstLick`). For this conversion the decoder spec requires
  **time** alignment to corridor entry, so I use the native imaging frames (no
  positional interpolation) — this is also what the reference does for its d′
  analyses ("these data points are computed from original estimated deconvolved
  traces without interpolation").
- Valid-sample mask used everywhere in the reference:
  `beh['ft_CorrSpc'][:nfr] & (beh['ft_move'][:nfr] > 0)`.

### Curation Steps

**Neuron curation rules** (from reference code):
1. Keep only neurons inside visual cortex: `iarea ∉ {-1, 7}`
   (`Get_density_map`: "exclude neurons from outside of visual cortex").
   Regions: V1(8), mHV(0,1,2,9), lHV(5,6), aHV(3,4).
2. No SNR/quality threshold is applied anywhere in the reference (Suite2p's own cell
   classifier has already run).

**Trial curation rules** (from reference code):
1. Only samples inside the 0–4 m texture region (`ft_CorrSpc`).
2. Only samples while the VR was moving (`ft_move > 0`).
3. Frames outside the behavioural record (`ft_trInd` NaN) are dropped, and behaviour
   is truncated to the number of imaging frames (`[:nfr]`).
4. Trials whose stimulus has no canonical `stim_id` are never used in any reference
   analysis.

## Step 5: Mapping Planning
**Status**: COMPLETE

### Session / trial / sample definition
- **Session** = one unique recording `(mname, datexp, blk)`; 89 sessions, ordered by
  (mouse, date, block).
- **Trial** = one corridor traversal, `ft_trInd == n`, restricted to
  `ft_CorrSpc & (ft_move > 0)`, i.e. the 0–4 m texture region while the VR was moving.
- **Alignment event** = trial start = corridor entry (`Trial_start_time[n]` /
  `StartFr[n]`), `off_start = 0.0 s`, `off_end = None` (variable-length trials).
- **Time bin** = one imaging frame, 314.85 ms (fs = 3.176 Hz).

### Key Decisions
1. **One session per unique recording, `stim_id` merged across experiment types.**
   The 142 `exp_info` records are views of 89 recordings; using them all would
   duplicate neural data 1.6×. Verified the behaviour payloads are identical.
2. **Samples: corridor (0–4 m) AND VR moving.** Directly from the paper ("only …
   inside the 0–4-m region", "excluded the data points in which the animal was not
   running") and from `Get_dprime_selective_neuron`'s `fr_valid = VRmove & isCorridor`.
   Also required by the decoder spec: the position output is 4 × 1 m bins covering
   exactly the 4 m texture region.
3. **Neurons: `iarea ∉ {-1, 7}`**, labelled V1 / mHV / lHV / aHV — the reference's own
   exclusion rule. Additionally drop neurons with zero variance over the retained
   samples (they carry no information and would produce 0/0 in z-scoring).
4. **Per-neuron z-scoring** across the retained samples of the session. Suite2p
   deconvolved amplitudes span 0 – ~8000 with wildly different per-neuron scales;
   the reference itself z-scores neurons before any population read-out
   (`get_kfold_reward_response`, `Get_sort_spk`). Without it the decoder's SVD
   initialisation and its L1-regularised linear projection are dominated by a handful
   of high-amplitude ROIs.
5. **Subsample to ≤2000 neurons per session**, stratified across the four visual
   regions in proportion to their sizes, with a fixed seed.
   *Why this is necessary*: keeping all 4,105,393 visual-cortex neurons would produce
   a **151.5 GB** array; training the provided decoder on it (200 epochs × 89 sessions)
   would require ~4.6 × 10¹⁵ FLOPs plus 30 TB of host→device traffic — on the order of
   100 h on the available L4, and the pickle alone would not be practical to ship or
   reload. 2000 is also the provided decoder's own `svd_max_neurons`, i.e. the point
   beyond which it already compresses each session with a random Gaussian projection.
   Stratifying by region keeps the recorded-area composition of every session.
   (The exact cap is chosen empirically in Step 7/8 by comparing decoder accuracy at
   1000 vs 2000 neurons.)
6. **Time variables are measured on the "running clock".** After the paper's
   non-running samples are removed, wall-clock elapsed time has a pathological tail
   (median trial duration 7.1 s, 99th pct 74 s, max 1765 s) produced entirely by the
   stops the paper excludes. I therefore accumulate time only over running frames:
   `τ[k] = dt · #{running frames ≤ k}`, and report
   `time_since_trial_start = τ(k) − τ(StartFr[n])`,
   `time_to_sound_cue = τ(SoundFr[n]) − τ(k)` (fractional frame indices interpolated
   onto τ). For the 88 % of trials without a long stop this is within ~0.4 s of
   wall-clock time; it removes the outliers and keeps the input on a scale the linear
   decoder can use.
7. **Stimulus output = the paper's canonical `stim_id` (7 classes).** This is the
   reference's own stimulus variable and is *role*-aligned across mice (leaf1 ≡ the
   rewarded exemplar), which is what makes a shared read-out across 19 mice and 3
   different texture sets (circle/leaf, rock/wood) meaningful. Using raw `WallName`
   would give mutually incompatible labels across mice; using only the two categories
   (circle vs leaf) would throw away the exemplar information the paper's Figs. 2–3
   are built on.
8. **`circle3` trials excluded (309 trials, 0.81 %, 4 sessions).** This wall has
   `stim_id = NaN` in *every* experiment-type view, i.e. the reference assigns it no
   role and excludes it from every analysis. Keeping it would add an 8th class present
   in 4/89 sessions, which the balanced-accuracy metric would weight as heavily as
   `leaf1`.
9. **Reward availability from `isRew`.** `utils.get_cat_id` identifies the rewarded
   wall as `WallName[isRew][0]`; a trial is "in the rewarded corridor" iff its wall is
   that wall. Unsupervised/naive sessions have `isRew` all-False → all zeros, which is
   correct (those mice were never rewarded, though they still heard the cue).
10. **Licking binned with `floor(LickFr)`**: frame *k* covers `[t_k, t_{k+1})`, so a
    lick at fractional frame index 140.945 belongs to frame 140.
11. **Speed quartiles computed globally** (over every retained sample of the whole
    dataset), not per session, so that class *q2* means the same thing in every
    session for the shared read-out.
12. **Day of training = days since that mouse's first imaging session** (0–92).
    `exp_info` only carries an explicit `days` field for a handful of records, so the
    session date is the only definition available for all 89 sessions.
13. **Trials must have ≥2 retained samples** (the observed minimum is 11, so this
    never fires; it is a guard).

`/app/convert_data.py`. Structure:

| Function | Purpose |

| `stratified_subsample` | ≤ `--max-neurons` neurons per session, quota per region ∝ region size, fixed seed |

| `_worker` / `multiprocessing.Pool` | 8 sessions processed in parallel |
| `show_processing` | 8-panel step-by-step figure + distribution figure per session |

Code inefficiencies identified and removed:
- naive version concatenated all imaging planes first (up to 7 GB copy) before masking;
  now each plane is **column-sliced before concatenation** (~2 GB), which also removes a
  full-size temporary.
- behaviour files were being re-read once per experiment-type record (142 reads of up to
  1 GB); now each `Beh_*.npy` is read exactly once and the 89 sessions are keyed out of it.
- only the behaviour fields actually needed are retained in memory (`BEH_FIELDS`), so the
  89-session registry is a few hundred MB instead of 6.6 GB.
- the global running-speed quartiles are computed once in the cheap behaviour-only pass,
  so the sample run and the full run share identical class boundaries.

Code speedups added: `multiprocessing.Pool(8)` over sessions (peak RSS ≈ 8 × 10 GB);
in-place z-scoring; `float32` throughout; per-trial `np.ascontiguousarray` views.

### Check 2 — comparison to the paper
The paper reports **no decoding analyses**: a full-text search of `paper.pdf` for
"decod", "accuracy", "chance" and "classifier" returns nothing. Its quantitative neural
results are selectivity indices (d′ ≥ 0.3), fractions of selective neurons per area,
coding-direction projections and sequence correlations — none of which is an accuracy.
There is therefore no published number to compare against, and the comparison table is
necessarily empty:

| Variable | Achieved validation accuracy | Paper's reported accuracy |
|---|---|---|
| stimulus | 0.861 | not reported (no decoding in the paper) |
| licking | 0.773 | not reported |
| position_bin | 0.897 | not reported |
| speed_bin | 0.574 | not reported |

What the paper *does* let us sanity-check is that all four variables should be
decodable from these populations, and by how much they should differ: it reports strong
stimulus selectivity in V1 and the HVAs (Fig. 1f–j), strong spatial/positional sequences
in the corridor (Fig. 2), a reward/licking-related ramping signal in the anterior HVAs
of task mice (Fig. 4), and locomotion-dependent modulation throughout. The ordering we
obtain (position ≈ stimulus > licking > speed) matches that picture: position and
texture identity are the two variables the paper shows to be most strongly and most
widely encoded, whereas the lick signal is reported only for the task cohort (28 of
89 sessions) and instantaneous running speed is the noisiest of the four.

### Check 3 — train vs validation gap
| Output | Train | Validation | Train/Val |
|---|---|---|---|
| stimulus | 0.998 | 0.861 | 1.16 |
| licking | 0.991 | 0.773 | 1.28 |
| position_bin | 1.000 | 0.897 | 1.12 |
| speed_bin | 0.940 | 0.574 | **1.64** |

Only `speed_bin` exceeds the 1.5× threshold. **Investigated with an ablation** rather
than assumed: the decoder learns a *separate* `nneurons × 100` projection for every
session — 200,000 parameters per session against ~6,500 training samples per session —
so it can memorise training timepoints. Halving the neuron count halves that capacity:

| neurons/session | speed_bin train | speed_bin validation | train/val |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1000 | 0.779 | 0.575 | 1.35 |
| 1500 | 0.867 | 0.571 | 1.52 |
| 2000 | 0.940 | 0.574 | 1.64 |

The **validation** accuracy is flat at 0.574 ± 0.002 across all three. The extra
training accuracy is therefore pure memorisation of the per-session projection and is a
property of the decoder's capacity, not of the data: if there were label leakage the
validation accuracy would rise with capacity, and if the conversion were misaligned the
validation accuracy would fall towards chance. Neither happens. The same ablation shows
there is no leakage in the other three outputs either (their validation accuracies move
smoothly and by small amounts with capacity).

### Check 4 — choosing the neuron cap (ablation on the full dataset)
Validation balanced accuracy, full 89-session dataset:

| neurons/session | stimulus | licking | position | speed | mean | pickle |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1000 | 0.825 | 0.810 | 0.845 | 0.575 | 0.764 | 3.3 GB |
| 1500 | 0.845 | 0.783 | 0.875 | 0.571 | 0.768 | ~4.9 GB |
| **2000** | **0.861** | **0.773** | **0.897** | **0.574** | **0.776** | **6.6 GB** |
| 3000 | 0.278 | 0.708 | 0.578 | 0.332 | 0.474 | ~9.8 GB |
| 4000 | 0.287 | 0.705 | 0.551 | 0.334 | 0.469 | 13.1 GB |

(The 1000/1500/3000/4000 rows and the z-scoring ablation below were measured on the
dataset before the 21-sample boundary fix of Step 10; that fix changes 21 of 815,506
samples, i.e. 0.0026 %, and the 2000-neuron run moved from 0.860/0.760/0.896/0.573 to
0.861/0.773/0.897/0.574 — within the ±0.01 run-to-run spread measured from two repeats
at the same setting. The rows are therefore directly comparable.)

2000 (which is also the decoder's own `svd_max_neurons`) is the best choice and was
kept. The collapse at ≥3000 is an optimisation failure of the *provided* decoder, not a
data problem: its L1 penalty `1e-4 · Σ|W|` grows with the neuron count while the
per-weight data gradient shrinks, so beyond ~2000 neurons the penalty drives the
projection to zero (train and validation accuracy collapse *together*, the signature of
under-fitting rather than over-fitting). Two independent runs at 2000 neurons gave
0.860/0.760/0.896/0.573 and 0.863/0.750/0.893/0.574, so the operating point is stable,
and the 1000 → 1500 → 2000 trend is smooth.

### Check 5 — z-scoring ablation
| neural scaling | stimulus | licking | position | speed | mean |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| per-neuron z-score (kept) | 0.861 | 0.773 | 0.897 | 0.574 | 0.776 |
| raw deconvolved amplitudes | 0.826 | 0.780 | 0.870 | 0.630 | 0.777 |

Accuracy is a wash (0.776 vs 0.777), so the choice was made on principle, not on the

every neuron and every session on the same scale (raw deconvolved amplitudes span
0 – ~8,000 with per-neuron scales differing by orders of magnitude), and it makes the
dataset insensitive to the decoder's input scaling. `convert_data.py --no-zscore`
reproduces the ablation.
